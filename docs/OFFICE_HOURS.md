# Office Hours: RoomQuest

Mode: startup diagnostic, pre-product. This unattended run uses the report's evidence as provisional answers; no founder interview or customer observation occurred.

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** The strongest behavior is 87 visible reviews across two Muksun products at a roughly $99 ticket. That proves some buyers pay for custom games, not that they want a home-as-map version. No deposit, retention, or reaction-video conversion evidence exists.
2. **Painful status quo:** A gift buyer searches lists, asks communities, buys redundant gaming gear, or commissions a generic personalized game. The cost is uncertainty and an emotionally flat gift, not an operational emergency. This is desire, not desperate B2B pain.
3. **Specific customer:** A social-first adult buying a $100–$250 milestone gift for a gamer partner who owns the standard hardware, has ten usable phone photos, and needs delivery within a week.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** A manually fulfilled $149 birthday escape-room build for one room, one recipient, and one of three fixed templates, preceded by a free quality-scored preview.
5. **Observation plan:** Recruit 20 buyers, watch each upload photos without guidance, record abandonment/reshoot reasons, deliver manually, and measure preview completion, deposit acceptance, recipient completion, refund requests, and opt-in reaction-video reach.
6. **Future-fit:** Better reconstruction makes supply cheaper, but also erodes technical differentiation. The durable asset would be a conversion dataset connecting photo-set traits, templates, QA decisions, occasions, and reaction performance—not the renderer.

## Premise challenge

- The business outcome is a memorable, shareable gift delivered reliably; “AI-generated 3D” is only a production method.
- Doing nothing leaves a mild but recurring gifting frustration. That cannot support poor execution or a long onboarding flow.
- Existing capture/runtime tools should be orchestrated, not rebuilt.

## Approaches considered

### A — Concierge validation (recommended)
Effort S; risk low. Sell one-room fixed-template builds manually and use the preview as the CTA. Fast evidence and limited engineering; capacity remains manual.

### B — Automated preview pipeline
Effort M; risk high. Integrate capture/reconstruction and automatically score scenes. Better funnel demonstration, but quality and privacy failures arrive before demand is proven.

### C — Full game platform
Effort XL; risk very high. User-authored rooms, editors, multiplayer, and reusable worlds. Stronger long-term software asset, but it ignores the narrow gift purchase and rebuilds mature tooling.

## Assignment

Offer 20 free one-room builds in exchange for candid workflow observation and optional reaction-video rights. Ask for a refundable $25 reservation from half the cohort. Continue only if at least five complete the photo set and at least two accept the price anchor.
